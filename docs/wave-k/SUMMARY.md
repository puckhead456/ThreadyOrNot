# Wave K in one page

You asked for knitting, cross-stitch and sewing to be as rich as crochet. About twenty helpers
built it over four sessions. **It is saved and on the live site as of 13 November.**
Crochet did not change.

## What each craft can do now

**Every craft**
- A full-screen working mode, like crochet's: big numbers, big buttons, the screen kept awake.
  A page-turner pedal or a clicker works too.
- A mark beside anything the app read from a pattern: ✓ matches, ≈ worked out, ≠ disagrees,
  ? nothing to check. Tap it to see why. When the app disagrees with the pattern, the "Use it"
  button steps back and asks once. It never stops you.
- Share a picture of any project.
- Save any project as a template.
- Your measurements, units and floss stash live in one place. They come back from a backup, with
  your theme and settings.
- Time spent is kept, with a streak. After five sessions you get a rough finish date.
- An optional reader for scanned pages. It downloads about 5 MB once, only when you ask.

**Knitting**
- "Work until it measures 4 in" is no longer a guess. The counter counts the stretch and waits
  for you to tap "I'm at the length". It can suggest a row count from your gauge, but uses it only
  if you say so.
- It shows the actual row a repeat lands on, not just "repeat the last 6 rounds".
- Cable panels keep counting through "continue in pattern". Lace and colourwork rows read from a
  chart now have stitch counts.
- "At the same time" shaping becomes a small checklist. "Work the left front the same, reversed"
  can be written out for you, each row marked as worked out by the app.
- Sleeves and socks come in pairs. On long rows one tap can count a whole repeat.
- A pattern book imports the one pattern you pick, with its own sizes and charts.
- A materials list, a simple drawing of each piece, notes on a row, a clicker setting.
- In 3D a sock's foot turns to face you, colourwork shows on the model, and a sweater's pieces
  can be seen sewn together.

**Cross-stitch**
- No more lost stitches. The Pac-Man chart used to drop 5,000 stitches. Now it reads all 10
  colours exactly. When the app cannot tell two colours apart, it asks you.
- A full-screen stitch mode. The big button shows the stitches as thread on fabric.
- Parking markers, page outlines, "mark this page done", find my place, row by row by page.
- Read a colour key from a picture. On your Po Ta Toes screenshot all 6 colours read right.
- The designer's own symbols, when they come through cleanly.
- New sheets: stitching time and goals, fabric and frame sizes, floss stash and shopping list,
  a time-lapse of your stitching, a framed picture, print what's left, join chart files.
- Works for diamond painting, needlepoint, filet crochet and fuse beads too.

**Sewing**
- A full-screen sewing mode you can read from the machine.
- Each step shows its own drawing from the booklet when the app is sure which one it is.
- A size suggestion from your measurements that quotes the designer's own advice. You choose.
- The import tells you what it missed: steps, sentences, picture-only pages.
- A cutting board by fabric and interfacing, quilt calculators, a fabric shopping list across
  projects, a timer, a glossary, and a fitting log with photos.
- Pattern sheets: a map of which pages to tape together, and which size layers to print.

## What is still weak (honestly)

- **Nothing has been tried on a real phone.** The 3D, the thread-on-fabric button and the picture
  readers have only run on a desktop computer.
- Knitting: a row-by-row check on real sweaters and socks found wrong lines (a repeating stretch
  showing the same row every time, a chart row past the end of its chart, one sock showing another
  sock's size). Those are fixed and now tested on the same patterns. Where the app cannot be sure
  which row a repeat means, it shows the pattern's own sentence. Still rough: three patterns import
  only partly (a saddle-shoulder cardigan, a cardigan's fronts, one sock's leg chart), and one
  sock's length estimate runs about 21 rounds high (it is marked ≈ and never used as a target).
- Knitting picture charts: 8 of the 9 in the new patterns are now found and measured. A hand-drawn
  intarsia chart is not.
- A sweater from a real pattern is not yet put together in 3D. Only test pieces are.
- Sewing drawings: on the sundress only 5 of 15 steps get their own drawing. Some patterns get
  no name where the app could not be sure of one.
- Cross-stitch: a chart split over several picture pages joined to the wrong size on the first real
  file. The app now says so. Phone photos of paper charts and small black-and-white symbol charts
  do not read yet.
- Only DMC colours. Anchor and other brands are not matched.

## What to try first on your phone

1. Open a cross-stitch chart and tap **Stitch mode**. Tap a few stitches. Does the thread look
   right, and does it feel quick?
2. Open a knitting sock in 3D. Count across the heel. Does the foot turn to you?
3. Import a sewing booklet and open **Sewing mode**. Can you read it from your machine?
4. Enter your measurements and open a garment's **Size & alterations**. Does the suggestion
   match what you would pick?
5. Take a screenshot of a colour key and use **Read a key from a picture**.
